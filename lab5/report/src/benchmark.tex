\section{Performance test}

\subsection{Method}

The test compares the construction of the generalized suffix tree together with the search for
the answer against the classical dynamic programming solution of the longest common substring
problem. The DP keeps only two rolling rows of length $|S_2|+1$ and runs in
$O(|S_1|\cdot|S_2|)$ time and $O(|S_2|)$ memory. The suffix tree is built in
$O((|S_1|+|S_2|) \log \sigma)$, where $\sigma \le 258$ is the size of the alphabet,
after which a single iterative traversal is performed.

On small sizes the benchmark compares the full answer sets of the two algorithms. On large sizes
the DP is skipped (when $(|S_1|+1)(|S_2|+1)$ exceeds $25 \cdot 10^6$ cells), since its quadratic
running time becomes too large.

\subsection{Analysis of the results}

Already on small tests the suffix tree is faster than the DP: for strings of length 4\,000 the
speedup is about \textbf{30 times}. As the size grows further, the DP becomes impractical because
of its quadratic running time, while the suffix tree keeps working within the limits. The
benchmark also checks correctness: on small tests the sets of substrings found by the tree and by
the DP coincide.
