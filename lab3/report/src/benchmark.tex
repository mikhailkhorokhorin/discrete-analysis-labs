\section{Performance research results}

This section presents the results of testing the PATRICIA tree compared with the standard
container \texttt{std::map}, as well as the analysis of memory consumption and the profiling of
the fixed program.

\subsection{RAM consumption analysis}

test set of $100\,000$ insertions, $100\,000$ lookups and $100\,000$ removals.

\subsection{Performance analysis with gprof}

For a detailed analysis of the execution time, the target \texttt{lab3\_profile} was built with the
\texttt{-pg} flag. The \texttt{gprof} report for $100\,000$ operations of each type showed the
following main lines (\texttt{make research} on the current version of the code).

\begin{table}[!ht]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{|l|r|r|}
\hline
\rowcolor{lightgray}
Function & Share of time, \% & Number of calls \\
\hline
\texttt{main} & 59.20 & -- \\
\hline
\texttt{PatriciaTrie::remove} & 16.33 & 100\,000 \\
\hline
\texttt{PatriciaTrie::find} & 12.25 & 100\,000 \\
\hline
\texttt{PatriciaTrie::insert} & 10.21 & 100\,000 \\
\hline
\texttt{PatriciaTrie::removeNodeWithoutSelfLoop} & 2.04 & 68\,956 \\
\hline
\texttt{PatriciaTrie::removeNodeWithSelfLoop} & 0.00 & 31\,044 \\
\hline
\texttt{normalizeKey} & 0.00 & 300\,000 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Profiling shows that most of the time (59\%) is spent in \texttt{main}, which reads and parses
the $300\,000$ input commands and runs the \texttt{std::map} part of the benchmark; the tree
operations take the rest. Removal is the most expensive PATRICIA operation, because after the
physical removal of a node the back edges have to be restored: about 69\% of the removals hit a
node without a self loop and need the more complex \texttt{removeNodeWithoutSelfLoop}.

\subsection{Comparison of the original and the fixed version}

The fixes did not change the asymptotic complexity of the tree operations: search, insertion and
removal still descend the tree by critical bits in the same way. The main benefit of the fixes is
correct handling of serialization errors and invalid input, and a fair benchmark in which the
lookup loop cannot be removed by the optimizer.
